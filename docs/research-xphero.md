# XP Hero combat: how it works, how it feels, what to learn

Reference brief prepared for Nir, checked 8.11.2026. Saved by Claude, lightly condensed. The game is a direction, not a spec (Nir, 8.11: "gathering like MyUniverse, combat like XP Hero").

## Central idea
Fighting feels like moving through a world and turning nearby danger into visible progress. Basic attacks are automatic near enemies, so attention goes to where to move, whom to approach, how long to stay, when to retreat. Shorthand: **low input effort, frequent combat feedback, strong sense of growing power.** [1, 3, 4]

## What the combat does
1. **Approach and let attacks work.** The hero swings automatically at nearby enemies, no button per swing. Auto-hunt exists too, but its activation rules are not established. Do not assume releasing the joystick starts it. [3, 4, 5, 6]
2. **Movement decides what you engage.** Fights happen in explorable map areas, between groups along a path. Players watch health and return to base to heal before dying. [3, 4, 7]
3. **Developed loadouts overlap.** Later footage: several weapons around the hero, arcs, projectiles, electric effects, many enemies hit at once. Slot rules, targeting and what is basic vs passive vs skill are not established. [1, 2, 7]
4. **Enemies resist differently.** Small enemies in groups; larger ones with visible health bars. A red ground lane appears before a large enemy charges along it (one example, not a rule). Progress is strongly power-driven, so do not overstate dodging. [1, 5, 7]
5. **Fighting feeds the next upgrade.** Fight → collect (coins, cargo fills up) → return to base, upgrade → fight stronger enemies. [3, 4, 5, 6, 7]

## How it feels (design analysis, not hands-on play)
- **You steer a source of damage**, not execute combos. Approach, stay, move on, retreat stay meaningful.
- **The game keeps answering you:** weapon arcs, impact flashes, floating numbers, health bars dropping, coins and cargo rising. Feedback is tied to damage and rewards; it communicates results.
- **Productive rhythm:** travel, contact a group, repeated hits, collect, move on. Base visits are a break and a reason to go back out.
- **Power has a visible payoff:** enemies that resisted you become easy. [4, 5, 8]
- **Relaxed, not motionless:** routine fights are easy; health pressure and attack warnings give reasons to reposition.
- **Cautions:** reviews report repetitive trips, grind and forced ads. Don't copy the progression or monetization wholesale. [1, 4, 6]

## Possible implications for Six Lanterns (options, not approved)
- Keep movement the central combat decision; existing auto-attack supports the feel.
- Make each attack's result readable: motion or projectile, short impact, health change, clear defeat and reward.
- Ordinary encounters = short satisfying damage-and-collect sequences; stronger enemies change the rhythm, not long health-bar grinds.
- Make upgrades noticeable in the next fight, not only as a number in a menu.
- Keep danger legible under the effects; XP Hero's late density could hurt phone readability.
- Do not infer six weapons, auto-hunt, a cargo limit, a new class system or a camera change.

**Target: moving into combat produces clear, frequent, satisfying results, while movement and visible danger still matter.**

## Evidence and limits
Gameplay inspected: [3] ~0:06-0:14, 0:21-0:26, 0:32-0:39, 0:46-0:51 (travel, sword, base, upgrade); [7] ~0:39-0:42, 1:04-1:10, 1:16-1:20 (overlapping attacks, cargo, red warning). Recordings are from 3.2025 and 11.2025. No frame data, latency, hit-stop, i-frames, targeting or damage formula claimed. Audio and haptics not evaluated. No hands-on play.
